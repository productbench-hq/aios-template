# Clarification Email Template

Use this structure for the follow-up email to the feature requester. The tone should be warm, collaborative, and genuinely appreciative of their idea.

```markdown
# Clarification Email: [Request Title]

**Subject:** Re: [Request Title] — Love the idea, a few questions

---

Hi [Name/Team],

## Appreciation

[1-2 sentences thanking them for the idea. Be specific about what's compelling — don't use generic praise. Reference a concrete aspect of their suggestion that shows you engaged with it thoughtfully.]

## What We Understood

Just to make sure we're on the same page, here's what we took away from your request:

[2-4 sentences paraphrasing the request in your own words. Restate the problem they're trying to solve, the proposed solution, and who it's for. This gives them a chance to correct any misunderstanding before you go further.]

## A Few Questions

To help us evaluate this properly, it would be great to get your thoughts on a few things:

### [Theme 1, e.g., "Understanding the need"]
[Questions drawn from 🔴 Blind spots and high-relevance criteria. Frame them as genuine curiosity, not challenges.]

1. [Question]
2. [Question]

### [Theme 2, e.g., "Scope and expectations"]
[Questions drawn from 🟡 Assumed criteria that are high-relevance. Frame as wanting to validate your thinking.]

3. [Question]
4. [Question]

### [Theme 3, e.g., "Practical considerations"]
[Questions about feasibility, resources, or constraints that would shape the approach.]

5. [Question]

## Closing

[1-2 sentences expressing genuine enthusiasm about exploring this further. Make them feel like a valued collaborator, not someone whose idea is being gatekept.]

Best,
[Your name]
```

## Guidelines for the Clarification Email

1. **Lead with warmth** — The requester put thought into this idea. Honor that.
2. **Paraphrase, don't parrot** — Restate the request in your own words to show comprehension and surface misunderstandings.
3. **Prioritize blind spots** — 🔴 Blind confidence items are the most important to ask about. We're making decisions without data here.
4. **Validate assumptions** — 🟡 Assumed items on high-relevance criteria deserve questions. Frame as "we think X, but want to check."
5. **Hide the framework** — Group questions by natural themes (need, scope, practicalities), not by criterion names. The requester doesn't need to know about the 10-criteria model.
6. **Keep it short** — Maximum 5-7 questions. More than that feels like a homework assignment.
7. **No leading questions** — Don't telegraph that you're skeptical. Ask open questions that could go either way.
8. **Close with energy** — End on a note that makes them want to reply, not dread it.
